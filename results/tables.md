## Two identical enterprise NVMe drives of one machine

Two Dell Ent NVMe v2 AGN RI U.2 3.84TB, firmware 2.3.0, PCIe 4.0 x4, the two members of a RAID0 of 512 KiB chunks, each client's I/O kept to its own member's chunks. Nine pairs and sixteen policies, in rounds: each round runs every policy once on every pair, the policies of a pair in a fresh random order. A run is kept when, in every second of it, the two drives saw no I/O but the replay's own and nobody else's processes ran beyond the quiet criterion ("quiet"), or when it failed the criterion only on other users' jobs, which ran on other cores ("shared"). A run that is neither is rejected and run again, up to four attempts. A round counts for a pair when every policy of the pair has a kept run in it or failed four attempts.

719 kept runs in the rounds counted: 605 quiet, 114 shared and 1 missing after four attempts. 32 attempts were rejected and run again. Missing: P1, round 5, no admission control. A round whose run with no admission control is missing gives its pair no ratio to that run, and still gives the comparisons between policies.

## The floor of hedging

With hedging alone a read still unanswered after its client's timeout H is copied to the other drive, so it returns no sooner than H plus one read there. Per round, the tail's client is the one with more of its reads above the pooled p99.9 of the run with no admission control, and the floor is its H plus the other drive's median read latency, over the pooled p99.9 (median over rounds). Capped: the pooled p99.9 of that run with every read capped at its client's H plus the other drive's median, over the pooled p99.9 (median, lowest to highest over rounds). Room taken: how much of the cut the floor allows hedging alone takes, (1 minus hedging alone) over (1 minus the floor). Microseconds, medians over rounds.

Hedging alone ends at or above the floor on every pair but S1 of the identical drives, S3 of the identical drives and S3 of the unlike drives. There the tail is split between the two clients and rests on a few reads, so a copy answered sooner than the other drive's median can beat the floor, and the floor is no bound.
